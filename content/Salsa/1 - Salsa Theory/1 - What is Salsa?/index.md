### What is Salsa?

#### Salsa Music
- **Origins and Evolution**: Emerged from mid-20th-century Afro-Cuban roots (son montuno, mambo, rumba, cha-cha-chá, guaracha) fused with Puerto Rican bomba/plena, African percussion, jazz, R&B, and rock; crystallized in 1960s-1970s New York City via Cuban/Puerto Rican diaspora after the 1959 Cuban Revolution.
- **Naming and Popularization**: Term "salsa" (Spanish for "sauce," implying a zesty blend) coined by Venezuelan radio host Phidias Danilo Escalona in 1960; boosted by Fania Records (founded 1964 by Johnny Pacheco and Jerry Masucci), which sold millions of records.
- **Key Characteristics**: 4/4 clave rhythm at 150-250 BPM; instrumentation includes congas, bongos, timbales, cowbell, piano montunos, brass sections; features sonero-style improvisations and lyrics on urban struggles, identity, and romance.
- **Pioneers and Icons**: Celia Cruz (1925-2003, "Queen of Salsa," e.g., "Quimbara"); Tito Puente (1923-2000, "El Rey," 100+ albums); Willie Colón (trombone innovator, social commentary); Héctor Lavoe (1946-1993, "El Cantante"); Rubén Blades (Panamanian lyricist, Grammy-winner); Eddie Palmieri (jazz-salsa pioneer); Ray Barretto (conga master).
- **Subgenres and Impact**: Includes salsa dura (hard-hitting, 1970s), romántica (ballad-focused, 1980s, e.g., Frankie Ruiz), and timba (Cuban funk-infused, 1990s, e.g., Los Van Van); global sales exceed 200 million records by 2020s; UNESCO recognized Cuban son as intangible heritage in 2012.

#### Salsa Dance
- **Origins and Evolution**: Partnered style synced to music's energy, with 3 steps per 4 beats (including pauses); derived from 1920s-1940s Cuban son/mambo/danzón (hip/shoulder isolations, turns) blended with U.S. swing/lindy hop/hustle post-WWII.
- **Key Styles**:
  - **New York-Style**: Linear, "on 2" break (emphasizing second beat), elegant; developed in 1970s Palladium era; codified by Eddie Torres.
  - **Los Angeles-Style**: Flashy, "on 1" timing, with acrobatics, dips, and lifts; popularized in 1980s by Vazquez Brothers.
  - **Cuban Casino**: Circular, often in rueda (group format with partner switches); originated in 1950s Havana; influenced by Pedro Oviedo.
  - **Colombian/Cali-Style**: Rapid footwork, pachanga/boogaloo roots; boomed in 1970s; known for world's fastest variations (300+ BPM); Cali dubbed "Salsa Capital."
- **Spread and Modern Forms**: Disseminated via migration, films (e.g., "Dirty Dancing," 1987), and festivals (e.g., Cali World Salsa Festival, 2005-present, 500k attendees); now in 80+ countries; includes social (nightclubs), competitive (World Salsa Championships, 2004-), performative (Broadway), and therapeutic elements; hybrids like salsaton (reggaeton fusion) and sensual (bachata influences) highlight ongoing cultural syncretism.
